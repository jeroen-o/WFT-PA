Wft Consumer Credit

For anyone who informs or advises on loans and credit. Practise for free with PE practice questions for Wft Consumer Credit, with an explanation for every answer.

Wft Consumer Credit at a glance

  • Who needs it: required for 1 professional qualification: Consumer Credit Adviser.
  • PE: you take one PE exam per professional qualification within the PE period, which runs until 31 March 2028 inclusive.
  • Exam terms: the final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD have been renewed for exams from 1 April 2026.
  • Current: CCD II from 20 November 2026: the revised Consumer Credit Directive will apply; the Dutch implementation act is still in preparation.
  • Practise: 7 multiple-choice questions with an explanation for every answer, free and without an account. Last updated 18 October 2026.

What is covered

  • Types of credit: revolving credit, personal loan, private lease, BNPL, overdraft
  • Creditworthiness assessment, lending standards and BKR registration
  • Information: European standard information and APR (JKP)
  • Right of withdrawal, early repayment and termination
  • Maximum credit fee (principle)
  • Wft and Wck framework
  • Debt assistance and payment problems
  • Duty of care, commission and refinancing
  • Wwft and GDPR in credit

What’s new in 2026

  • New CDFD exam terms from 1 April 2026. The final objectives and exam terms for this module have been renewed and apply to exams from 1 April 2026. The quiz questions are aligned with them.
  • CCD II from 20 November 2026. The revised Consumer Credit Directive will apply; the Dutch implementation act is still in preparation. Among other things, BNPL and rent or lease with a purchase option come under the credit regime.
  • Stricter creditworthiness assessment under CCD II. Credit may only be granted if the creditworthiness assessment shows that the consumer is likely to be able to meet their obligations. There will also be stricter requirements for advertising and pre-contractual information.

  2. Drawing up the risk analysis for the advice
  3. Advising (and where applicable intermediating) a suitable solution
  4. Managing and keeping the advice up to date

Example questions Wft Consumer Credit

This is what the quiz questions look like. In the quiz the answers appear in random order.

Example question 1 exam term 1a.3

Gerrit Bos (58) has a revolving credit with a limit of €4,000.00. As a result of a series of unexpected expenses, €5,500.00 has been outstanding for well over two months. The lender has permitted the drawings above the limit. Gerrit has not yet received any notification about this. What must the lender do?

  • Automatically raise the limit to the amount drawn
  • Inform the consumer about the overrun, the interest and the costs
  • Let the overrun continue tacitly without telling the consumer anything
  • Report the credit and the overrun to the AFM immediately
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: Inform the consumer about the overrun, the interest and the costs. In the case of a significant overrun lasting longer than a month, the consumer must be informed without delay.

  • Let the overrun continue tacitly without telling the consumer anything: The consumer must actually be informed in order to prevent further build-up of debt.
  • Automatically raise the limit to the amount drawn: A limit increase requires a new creditworthiness assessment and cannot be done automatically.
  • Report the credit and the overrun to the AFM immediately: Individual overruns are not reported to the AFM.

Example question 2 exam term 1h.5

Frank van Dijk (46) is divorced and pays €600.00 a month in spousal maintenance to his ex-wife. He rents an apartment and works full time as an IT administrator. He wants to borrow €10,000.00 to furnish his new home. He asks whether the maintenance is actually relevant to the application. How does the maintenance count in the assessment?

  • As a fixed charge that reduces the room for a monthly instalment
  • As extra income, because he can deduct the maintenance paid for tax purposes
  • It does not, because maintenance is a private arrangement between the ex-partners
  • Only if his ex-partner gives permission to use the data
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: As a fixed charge that reduces the room for a monthly instalment. He has to pay the maintenance every month; that amount is not available for interest and repayment.

  • It does not, because maintenance is a private arrangement between the ex-partners: A private arrangement can still be a hard payment obligation that reduces his repayment capacity.
  • As extra income, because he can deduct the maintenance paid for tax purposes: Any tax benefit does not turn an expense into income; on balance it remains a charge.
  • Only if his ex-partner gives permission to use the data: He is the applicant; his own charges belong in the assessment, regardless of his ex-partner's permission.

Example question 3 exam term 1c.2

Nadia (39) is a single mother of two children and has been in an amicable debt assistance process via her municipality for six months. Her washing machine and fridge break down. She applies to an online provider for a €1,500.00 loan for new appliances. She has not informed her debt counsellor about it. What is correct?

  • New credit runs counter to the process and can jeopardise the arrangement
  • That is fine, because the amicable process is voluntary
  • That is mandatory, so that creditors in the process can be paid faster
  • The municipality must in that case provide the loan itself
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: New credit runs counter to the process and can jeopardise the arrangement. In a debt arrangement the debtor may as a rule not incur new debts; new credit undermines the agreement with creditors.

  • That is mandatory, so that creditors in the process can be paid faster: New debts do not solve the problem and are not part of an amicable arrangement.
  • That is fine, because the amicable process is voluntary: A voluntary arrangement also contains agreements that exclude new debts.
  • The municipality must in that case provide the loan itself: The municipality has no duty to provide extra credit during the process.
