Wft Non-life Insurance (Commercial) at a glance

  • Who needs it: required for 1 professional qualification: Commercial Non-life Insurance Adviser.
  • PE: you take one PE exam per professional qualification within the PE period, which runs until 31 March 2028 inclusive.
  • Exam terms: the final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD have been renewed for exams from 1 April 2026.
  • Current: AI Act and DORA: they affect business clients and their insurance needs, including for cyber and IT risks.
  • Practise: 7 multiple-choice questions with an explanation for every answer, free and without an account. Last updated 18 October 2026.

What is covered

  • Commercial risk inventory
  • Fire, buildings, inventory and goods
  • Business interruption and indemnity period
  • Liability: AVB, product, employer, professional
  • Directors’ liability (D&O) and CAR
  • Transport, fleet and cyber
  • Collective contracts, delegated authority, market policies and co-insurance
  • Insurance law: duty of disclosure, first and second risk
  • IDD, Wwft and sanctions legislation

What’s new in 2026

  • New CDFD exam terms from 1 April 2026. The final objectives and exam terms for this module have been renewed and apply to exams from 1 April 2026. The quiz questions are aligned with them.
  • AI Act and DORA. They affect business clients and their insurance needs, including for cyber and IT risks.
  • New Product Liability Directive. EU Directive 2024/2853 must be transposed into Dutch law by 9 December 2026 and applies to products placed on the market after that date. Software, including AI systems, then counts as a product. Discuss with clients who make software or use it in their products whether their liability cover matches.
  • Sanctions legislation. Remains a standing point of attention for business clients and foreign interests.

Example questions Wft Non-life Insurance (Commercial)

This is what the quiz questions look like. In the quiz the answers appear in random order.

Example question 1 exam term 1i.24

Sanne de Boer is an account manager at wholesaler Verhoef in Breda, a company with sixty employees. She visits customers in her own car and receives a mileage allowance. On the way to a customer she is forced off the road by a driver who remains unknown, and suffers injury and damage to her car. Verhoef has not arranged accident or passenger insurance for such journeys. On what ground can Verhoef nevertheless be liable for her loss?

  • Not at all, because for a business journey in her own car the employer is never liable for her loss.
  • On the ground of good employership, because the employer must provide proper insurance.
  • On the ground of product liability, because the car was used for Verhoef's work.
  • On the ground of the WAM policy of the company cars, which also insures employees' own cars.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: On the ground of good employership, because the employer must provide proper insurance.. According to established case law, the employer must provide suitable insurance for employees who take part in traffic for their work; if it is missing, the employer may have to bear the loss itself.

  • On the ground of the WAM policy of the company cars, which also insures employees' own cars.: A WAM policy only covers the vehicles insured under it and moreover does not compensate the driver's own loss.
  • On the ground of product liability, because the car was used for Verhoef's work.: Product liability concerns defective products and has no relevance here.
  • Not at all, because for a business journey in her own car the employer is never liable for her loss.: Precisely for business journeys the employer can be liable on the ground of good employership, even in her own car.

Example question 2 exam term 1i.19

At printing firm Van Beek in Zutphen, with thirty employees, a fire breaks out on Sunday night in the paper store. The fire brigade extinguishes the fire, but the roof has partly collapsed and the extinguishing water stands among the printing presses. Still during the night, a salvage coordinator arrives on site at the fire brigade's request. Owner Harm van Beek asks what he is there to do and who pays for it. What is correct?

  • He establishes whether the fire was caused by the owner's intent or negligence in the company.
  • He sells the damaged stock immediately to secure the residual value for the insurer concerned.
  • He takes measures on behalf of the insurers at once to limit further loss; the costs are as a rule theirs.
  • He works on Harm's instructions, who must advance the costs himself and claim them later from the insurer.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: He takes measures on behalf of the insurers at once to limit further loss; the costs are as a rule theirs.. Salvage provides first aid after the fire, such as covering, securing and protecting machines; the insurers as a rule bear these costs.

  • He sells the damaged stock immediately to secure the residual value for the insurer concerned.: Salvage here is about limiting further loss after the fire, not about selling remnants.
  • He establishes whether the fire was caused by the owner's intent or negligence in the company.: Investigation of the cause belongs to the loss adjuster's work and possibly the police, not to the salvage coordinator.
  • He works on Harm's instructions, who must advance the costs himself and claim them later from the insurer.: The salvage service works for the insurers; the insured does not as a rule have to advance the costs.

Example question 3 exam term 1c.16

Machine builder Van der Meer in Heerenveen sells a packaging machine to a buyer in Germany. The contract is concluded on the basis of the Incoterm EXW (ex works). The German buyer has the machine collected by its own carrier. On the way the lorry tips over and the machine is badly damaged. Who has the main interest in a transport insurance during the carriage?

  • Seller and buyer each half, because EXW divides the risk.
  • The buyer, because the risk passes as soon as the machine is ready at the seller's premises.
  • The carrier, which is the only one with an insurable interest in the cargo.
  • The seller, because it remains owner until the machine has been delivered in Germany.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: The buyer, because the risk passes as soon as the machine is ready at the seller's premises.. Under EXW the seller has fulfilled its obligations once the goods are made available at its premises; the transport risk lies with the buyer.

  • The seller, because it remains owner until the machine has been delivered in Germany.: Under EXW the risk in fact already passes from the seller to the buyer.
  • The carrier, which is the only one with an insurable interest in the cargo.: The carrier insures its liability; the interest in the cargo itself lies with the buyer.
  • Seller and buyer each half, because EXW divides the risk.: A shared risk is not a feature of EXW.
