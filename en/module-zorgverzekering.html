Wft Health Insurance at a glance

  • Who needs it: required for 1 professional qualification: Health Insurance Adviser.
  • PE: you take one PE exam per professional qualification within the PE period, which runs until 31 March 2028 inclusive.
  • Exam terms: the final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD have been renewed for exams from 1 April 2026.
  • Current: Annual changes on 1 January: premiums, the mandatory deductible, the basic package and the healthcare allowance are set anew each year.
  • Practise: 7 multiple-choice questions with an explanation for every answer, free and without an account. Last updated 18 October 2026.

CDFD exam terms from 1 April 2026

The final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD for this module are divided into tasks:

  1. Gathering the client’s data
  2. Drawing up the risk analysis for the advice
  3. Advising (and where applicable intermediating) a suitable solution
  4. Supporting the management of the health and/or medical expenses insurance(s)
  5. Supporting claims handling

Example questions Wft Health Insurance

This is what the quiz questions look like. In the quiz the answers appear in random order.

Example question 1 exam term 2e.4

Mrs Visser (52) is a lawyer in The Hague and has suffered from a herniated disc for years. She wants to be operated on by a specialist at a private clinic with which her current insurer has no contract. She currently has a benefits-in-kind policy and wants to change policy on 1 January. She wants to pay as little as possible herself for the operation. Which policy type best fits her wish?

  • A benefits-in-kind policy
  • A budget policy
  • A policy with a voluntary deductible
  • A reimbursement policy
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: A reimbursement policy. A reimbursement policy reimburses care from a provider of choice up to a market-conform tariff, even without a contract with the clinic.

  • A budget policy: A budget policy has few contracts and often reimburses non-contracted care at a lower rate.
  • A benefits-in-kind policy: With benefits in kind she generally receives a lower reimbursement for non-contracted care.
  • A policy with a voluntary deductible: A voluntary deductible lowers the premium, but increases her own payment.

Example question 2 exam term 1l.10

Mrs De Groot (70) is a widow and moves to an apartment on the Costa Blanca in Spain. She receives only AOW (state pension) and a Dutch pension. Because she no longer lives in the Netherlands, she is no longer insured under the Wlz and her insurance obligation under the Zvw lapses. She has rheumatism and wants to stay in treatment in Spain. How does she arrange her care?

  • She is automatically insured in Spain, without paying a contribution
  • She keeps her ordinary Dutch basic insurance
  • She registers via the CAK as a treaty beneficiary (verdragsgerechtigde) and pays a treaty contribution
  • She registers with the Benefits Administration (Dienst Toeslagen) for a healthcare contribution
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: She registers via the CAK as a treaty beneficiary (verdragsgerechtigde) and pays a treaty contribution. Treaty beneficiaries receive care in their country of residence at the expense of the Netherlands; the CAK arranges the registration and the contribution.

  • She keeps her ordinary Dutch basic insurance: Without Wlz insurance she is no longer subject to the insurance obligation and cannot keep Zvw insurance.
  • She is automatically insured in Spain, without paying a contribution: Because she has only a Dutch benefit, the Netherlands is responsible; for this she pays a contribution.
  • She registers with the Benefits Administration (Dienst Toeslagen) for a healthcare contribution: The Benefits Administration implements allowances, not the registration of treaty beneficiaries.

Example question 3 exam term 1l.13

Tim (37) has multiple sclerosis and receives physiotherapy that falls under his basic insurance. He has a reimbursement policy. After moving to Zwolle he chooses a physiotherapist who has no contract with his insurer. He wonders what that means for payment. What can happen to Tim in this situation?

  • He may have to pay the invoice himself first and then claim it from his insurer
  • He must first ask permission from the National Health Care Institute
  • He must switch to a benefits-in-kind policy in order to be allowed to claim
  • He receives nothing reimbursed, because the physiotherapist has no contract
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: He may have to pay the invoice himself first and then claim it from his insurer. With reimbursement Tim is free in his choice, but a non-contracted provider may send the invoice to him, which he then claims.

  • He receives nothing reimbursed, because the physiotherapist has no contract: A reimbursement policy in fact also reimburses care from non-contracted providers, up to the market-conform tariff.
  • He must first ask permission from the National Health Care Institute: The National Health Care Institute does not decide on individual reimbursements.
  • He must switch to a benefits-in-kind policy in order to be allowed to claim: Claiming in fact belongs to the reimbursement policy.
