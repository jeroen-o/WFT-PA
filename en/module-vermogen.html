Wft Wealth at a glance

  • Who needs it: required for 3 professional qualifications: Mortgage Credit Adviser, Wealth Adviser, Pensions Adviser.
  • PE: you take one PE exam per professional qualification within the PE period, which runs until 31 March 2028 inclusive.
  • Exam terms: the final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD have been renewed for exams from 1 April 2026.
  • Current: Box 3: the Box 3 Transitional Act applies until a new system takes effect; legal redress following the “Christmas ruling” (Kerstarrest) is also under way.
  • Practise: 7 multiple-choice questions with an explanation for every answer, free and without an account. Last updated 18 October 2026.

What is covered

  • Wealth accumulation and financial planning
  • Saving and the deposit guarantee scheme
  • Investing: shares, bonds, funds, risk and diversification
  • Investment insurance, bank savings and annuities (lijfrente)

What’s new in 2026

  • New CDFD exam terms from 1 April 2026. The final objectives and exam terms for this module have been renewed and apply to exams from 1 April 2026. The quiz questions are aligned with them.
  • Box 3. The Box 3 Transitional Act applies until a new system takes effect; legal redress following the “Christmas ruling” (Kerstarrest) is also under way. Work with the rules of the relevant tax year.
  • Crypto: MiCA and DAC8. Since 30 December 2024, providers of crypto services in the EU need a licence under the MiCA Regulation; in the Netherlands the AFM grants it. A licence does not protect against price losses and there is no investor compensation scheme for crypto. Since 1 January 2026, crypto service providers in the EU pass information about their clients to the tax authorities (DAC8).
  • EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (AMLR) from 10 July 2027. The customer due diligence rules will sit in a directly applicable EU regulation.

Example questions Wft Wealth

This is what the quiz questions look like. In the quiz the answers appear in random order.

Example question 1 exam term 1k.5

Sandra Mulder (38) is single, works as a nurse and earns €3,900 gross per month. She wants to invest €250 per month for 25 years. Her bank offers an active fund with ongoing charges of 1.5% per year; a comparable index fund costs 0.2% per year. She thinks such a small difference makes little difference. With an equal gross return, what is the effect on her final capital?

  • The active fund always yields more, because management pays for itself
  • The costs affect the return only in the first year
  • The effect is negligible because the percentages are small
  • Through compound interest, the more expensive fund gives a considerably lower final capital
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: Through compound interest, the more expensive fund gives a considerably lower final capital. The cost difference is deducted every year and can then no longer earn a return; over 25 years this adds up considerably.

  • The effect is negligible because the percentages are small: Small percentages per year have a large influence over 25 years because of the compounding effect.
  • The active fund always yields more, because management pays for itself: There is no guarantee that active management earns back the higher costs; here the gross return is equal.
  • The costs affect the return only in the first year: Ongoing charges are charged again every year.

Example question 2 exam term 1a.7

Mrs Van Rijn (56) is a State Secretary and wants to have €500,000 managed by a portfolio manager. She is married, has two adult children and wants to use the assets in due course to supplement her pension. According to her, the money comes from an inheritance and the sale of a home. The manager starts customer due diligence. What must the institution in any case do under the Wwft?

  • Carry out enhanced customer due diligence, including the source of wealth and funds
  • Only make and keep a copy of her passport
  • Refuse her as a client, because a PEP may not become a client
  • Settle for simplified due diligence because of her public office
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: Carry out enhanced customer due diligence, including the source of wealth and funds. A State Secretary is a politically exposed person; enhanced due diligence is then mandatory, including into the origin of wealth and funds.

  • Refuse her as a client, because a PEP may not become a client: A PEP may become a client; stricter requirements do apply.
  • Settle for simplified due diligence because of her public office: A public office actually increases the risk and calls for enhanced due diligence.
  • Only make and keep a copy of her passport: Identification alone is insufficient; for a PEP more investigation is needed.

Example question 3 exam term 1m.7

Wouter (64) and Marjan (62) de Graaf have €900,000 in investments. They want to give their daughter Eva an amount as a gift in order to limit inheritance tax later, but want to keep the money themselves for the time being for their old age. They therefore opt for a gift on paper (schenking op papier). Eva agrees. What is needed for this acknowledgement of debt to be recognised for tax purposes?

  • A notarial deed and interest of at least 6% actually paid annually
  • A notarial deed; interest is not needed within the family
  • A notification of the gift to Wouter and Marjan's bank
  • A private letter, signed by the parents and Eva
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: A notarial deed and interest of at least 6% actually paid annually. A gift on paper is recognised if it is recorded by a notary and the parents genuinely pay at least 6% interest annually.

  • A private letter, signed by the parents and Eva: A private deed is not sufficient for a gift on paper.
  • A notarial deed; interest is not needed within the family: Without actually paid interest of at least 6%, the debt is not recognised on death.
  • A notification of the gift to Wouter and Marjan's bank: A notification to the bank plays no role in the tax recognition.
