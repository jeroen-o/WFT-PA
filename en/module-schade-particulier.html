Wft Non-life Insurance (Personal) at a glance

  • Who needs it: required for 2 professional qualifications: Commercial Non-life Insurance Adviser, Personal Non-life Insurance Adviser.
  • PE: you take one PE exam per professional qualification within the PE period, which runs until 31 March 2028 inclusive.
  • Exam terms: the final objectives and exam terms of the CDFD have been renewed for exams from 1 April 2026. One exam term for this module was changed.
  • Current: No major change in the law identified: the framework remains Book 7 of the Civil Code (insurance contract), the IDD and the Wft conduct rules.
  • Practise: 7 multiple-choice questions with an explanation for every answer, free and without an account. Last updated 18 October 2026.

What is covered

  • Insurance law: duty of disclosure, premium, compensation, underinsurance, limitation
  • Motor vehicles: WAM, third-party liability, comprehensive cover, no-claims bonus (bonus-malus)
  • Home and contents: rebuilding value, replacement value, perils and exclusions
  • Personal liability (AVP)
  • Legal expenses, travel and cancellation
  • Claims handling and expert assessment
  • Terrorism risk, natural disasters and flooding
  • Duty of care, IDD and Kifid

What’s new in 2026

  • New CDFD exam terms from 1 April 2026. The final objectives and exam terms for this module have been renewed and apply to exams from 1 April 2026. The quiz questions are aligned with them.
  • Changed exam term. Exam term 1d.9 was changed on 1 April 2026: more room for questions about passenger accident insurance (OVI) and passenger damage insurance (SVI), the differences between the two, and which form fits the client’s needs.
  • No major change in the law identified. The framework remains Book 7 of the Civil Code (insurance contract), the IDD and the Wft conduct rules. Policy conditions differ per insurer; always check the current conditions.

Example questions Wft Non-life Insurance (Personal)

This is what the quiz questions look like. In the quiz the answers appear in random order.

Example question 1 exam term 1d.2

Mr Jansen has a four-year-old car, insured with WA and limited casco. While parking in a car park he drives into a concrete pillar. The bumper and a door are damaged and there is no counterparty. He asks his adviser whether his insurance covers the damage to his car. What is the correct answer?

  • No, damage from a collision of one's own making is covered only under a full casco insurance.
  • Only if he reported the damage to the police within 24 hours.
  • Yes, limited casco covers all damage to one's own car for which no counterparty can be claimed against.
  • Yes, the WA insurance covers the damage, because the pillar belongs to someone else.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: No, damage from a collision of one's own making is covered only under a full casco insurance.. Limited casco covers, among other things, fire, theft, storm and windscreen damage, but not damage from a collision of one's own making.

  • Yes, limited casco covers all damage to one's own car for which no counterparty can be claimed against.: Limited casco covers only the named events; a collision caused by one's own fault is not part of it.
  • Yes, the WA insurance covers the damage, because the pillar belongs to someone else.: The WA insurance covers the damage to the pillar of the car park's owner, not the damage to his own car.
  • Only if he reported the damage to the police within 24 hours.: A police report is not a condition for cover of collision damage, and limited casco does not cover this damage.

Example question 2 exam term 1d.5

Lars has a racing bike worth €3,500. The bike is co-insured on his home contents policy and he also has a separate bicycle insurance. Both policies provide that they offer no cover if the damage is also insured elsewhere. The bike is stolen from his locked shed. What applies?

  • Lars receives the value twice, because he has paid premium twice.
  • Neither insurer pays, because each refers to the other.
  • Only the insurer with the oldest policy pays the full damage to Lars.
  • The clauses cancel each other out; both insurers bear the damage together.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: The clauses cancel each other out; both insurers bear the damage together.. If both policies contain such a clause, they cancel each other out; Lars has his damage reimbursed and the insurers divide it among themselves.

  • Neither insurer pays, because each refers to the other.: The law prevents the insured from being left empty-handed by two clauses referring to each other.
  • Only the insurer with the oldest policy pays the full damage to Lars.: A ranking by policy start date does not apply here; the insurers bear the damage together.
  • Lars receives the value twice, because he has paid premium twice.: The indemnity principle prohibits the insured from profiting from damage; he has his damage reimbursed once.

Example question 3 exam term 1d.20

Jeroen (42) has a personal accident insurance. While moving a heavy cupboard he feels a sharp pain in his back; it later turns out he has a herniated disc. A month later he slips on a wet staircase and breaks his ankle, leaving him with permanent injury. He reports both events to his insurer. Which event most clearly meets the concept of accident in most accident policies?

  • Both events, because both led to injury.
  • Neither, because they took place in and around his own home.
  • The fall on the staircase: sudden violence coming from outside, with medically determinable injury.
  • The herniated disc, because it arose suddenly while lifting the cupboard.
Show the answer and explanation

Correct: The fall on the staircase: sudden violence coming from outside, with medically determinable injury.. In most policies an accident is a sudden, involuntary effect of external violence on the body with medically determinable injury; the fall meets that.

  • The herniated disc, because it arose suddenly while lifting the cupboard.: A herniated disc as a rule has an internal cause; in most policies it is not an accident or is expressly excluded.
  • Both events, because both led to injury.: Injury alone is not enough; there must be sudden violence coming from outside on the body.
  • Neither, because they took place in and around his own home.: A personal accident insurance in most policies applies around the clock, so also at home.
